# Brewing Guide

## General tips

Use fresh, filtered water and heat it just once; reboiled water tastes flat. Use about 2 to 3 grams of leaf (one heaped teaspoon, or one Bamboo Tea Scoop) for every 200 ml of water. Warm your cup or teapot with hot water first. Always remove the leaves at the end of the steep, or the tea will turn bitter.

## Black tea

Use water just off the boil, around 95 °C. Steep for 3 to 4 minutes; first flush teas such as Ilam First Flush are best at 2 to 3 minutes, since they turn astringent if steeped too long. Breakfast blends and autumn flush teas can take milk; first flush and Golden Tips are best plain.

## Green tea

Use cooler water, around 75 to 80 °C, which is boiled water left to stand for 3 to 4 minutes. Steep for 2 minutes. Water that is too hot, or a steep that is too long, makes green tea bitter. Jasmine Green Pearls need about 3 minutes for the pearls to open.

## Oolong tea

Use water at 85 to 95 °C: cooler for light oolongs such as Mist Valley Oolong, hotter for roasted oolongs such as Roasted Hill Oolong. For a mug, steep for 3 to 4 minutes. In a gaiwan, use more leaf (about 5 grams for 150 ml) and steep for 30 to 60 seconds, adding 15 seconds for each further steep. Oolong can be steeped three to five times.

## White tea

Use water at 80 to 85 °C and steep for 3 to 5 minutes. Silver Needle is very forgiving and can steep longer for a sweeter cup without turning bitter. White teas can be steeped two or three times.

## Herbal infusions

Use boiling water and steep for 5 to 7 minutes; herbs need longer than tea leaves to release their flavour. Herbal infusions do not turn bitter if left longer. For iced Tulsi Lemongrass, brew it double strength and pour it over ice.

## Masala chai on the stove

For two cups: bring 1 cup of water to the boil with 2 heaped teaspoons of Classic Masala Chai or Cardamom Chai. Simmer for 2 minutes, then add 1 cup of milk and sugar to taste. Bring it back to the boil, let it rise once, lower the heat and simmer for another 2 minutes. Strain into cups.

## Storing your tea

Keep tea in its resealable pouch or an airtight tin, away from light, heat, moisture and strong smells. Do not refrigerate it. Most teas stay fresh for about a year once opened; green teas are best within six months.
